---
title: 4. Enter Recovery
description: Cmd+R on Intel, power-hold on Apple Silicon — timing matters on both.
---

<Tabs items={['Intel', 'Apple Silicon']}>
  <Tab value="Intel">

<Steps>
<Step>
Turn the Mac on (or restart it) and **immediately** press and hold **Command (⌘) + R** — start holding right after pressing power, before or during the startup chime, not after the screen lights up.
</Step>
<Step>
Keep holding both keys until the **Apple logo or a spinning globe** appears, then release. Don't let go at the chime — too early and it boots normally.
</Step>
<Step>
Wait for the **macOS Utilities** window to load.
</Step>
</Steps>

Related combinations, held the exact same way — press immediately, release at the Apple logo/globe:

| Keys | What you get |
| --- | --- |
| **⌘R** | Built-in Recovery; the latest macOS that was installed on this Mac |
| **⌥⌘R** | Internet Recovery; upgrades to the latest macOS this Mac supports |
| **⇧⌥⌘R** | Internet Recovery; the macOS the Mac shipped with (or closest still available) |
| **⌥** | Startup Manager, the boot-device picker — see [Step 5](/docs/steps/disk-and-volume) |

<Callout type="warn">
  If a padlock prompt appears instead of the Apple logo, that's a firmware password — go to
  [Step 3](/docs/steps/firmware-password).
</Callout>

  </Tab>
  <Tab value="Apple Silicon">

<Steps>
<Step>
Press and hold the power button until **"Loading startup options"** appears.
</Step>
<Step>
Release the power button.
</Step>
<Step>
Click **Options**, then **Continue**.
</Step>
<Step>
If prompted, select a volume and enter the admin password.
</Step>
</Steps>

The screen you land on at step 3 is the boot-device picker — the Apple Silicon equivalent of Startup Manager.

  </Tab>
</Tabs>
